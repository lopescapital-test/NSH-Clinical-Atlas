§1 · The Core Idea
The two-dimension framing

The protocol has two independent axes.

Profile answers what kind of case is this? — driven by the child's clinical presentation. Reflex burden, biomedical drivers, hemisphericity, regression history, immune-mediated activity, structural insult. Profile is durable: it changes only as a child fundamentally changes.

Intensity answers how much daily protocol does this case need, and can the family execute it? — driven by clinical demand AND family bandwidth. Intensity is tunable: a child can move HIGH → MID as they advance, or MID → HIGH if the clinician needs to push harder.

These are independent. A complex Profile B child can run on MID intensity if the family can't sustain HIGH. A modal Profile D child can occasionally run HIGH if the clinician is pushing a specific advancement window. Treating profile and intensity as a single combined "level" loses this flexibility — and loses what Dr. Kyle does intuitively when he prescribes.

Update v0.3: Profiles E and F — added by Dr. Kyle in May 2 review and pending data — are now empirically populated. The framework's six-cell structure is fully justified by the cohort. Every profile now has at least one real case backing it.

What this unlocks

For the Discovery Call: concierge captures observable signals and triages a family into one of 6 profiles. Edge cases (complex Profile B, ambiguous presentations, suspected PANS flares) get flagged for MD-led intake.

For the prescription: profile determines the contents of the protocol (which exercises, which devices, which biomedical workups). Intensity determines the dosing (how many minutes daily, how many exercises, how many reps).

For the tier mapping: support tier (Foundation / Accelerated / Immersive) is the third dimension — independent of both — that the family chooses based on their need for clinician scaffolding around the prescribed protocol.

How intensity differs from support tier

Intensity (LIGHT/MID/HIGH) is a clinical prescription — Dr. Kyle decides how much daily protocol the case needs. Support tier (Foundation/Accelerated/Immersive) is a family choice — how much clinician scaffolding they want around that prescription.

This is why the existing tier model in v0.5 works cleanly. The "personalized" piece happens through profile + intensity + BRB laterality. The "tiered support" piece is independent of all three.

What the empty cells reveal

  • Profiles A, B, E never run LIGHT. Heavy reflex work, complex multi-pathway demands, and ABI rebuild work all require moderate-to-high dosing minimum. A family unable to sustain MID is a poor fit for the home model in these profiles.

The default assumption from this matrix: if you don't yet have a confirmed profile, MID intensity is the safe starting point. It fits 26 of 41 cases (63%) and is recoverable in either direction — escalate to HIGH if the case warrants, or pull back to LIGHT if family bandwidth requires.
§6 · Patterns · NEW

Protocol signature elements

Recurring sub-protocols that cross profile boundaries. These are not profile-specific — they're modular building blocks that get added or omitted based on case-specific needs. v0.3 formalizes them so they can be referenced by name in V1 prescription generation.

Element 1 · Cross-cutting
Vibration-Speech Pairing Matrix
Maps cranial nerves V/VII/IX/X/XII to phoneme production with daily word lists and core sound sets (ka-ka-ka · la-la-la · ma-ma-ma). Daily speech work paired with vibration stimulation at relevant CN distribution. Not a Stage 4-5 add-on — it's now a first-class daily protocol element.
Appears in: Anaya, Ellis, Jimmy W, Sysha, Ted, Michael S, Michael L (7 cases) · spans Profiles B, D, F
Element 2 · Profile B/F
qEEG-Driven Cortical Targeting
Laser placements at named EEG sites (F3/F4, F7/F8, T3/T4, T5/T6, P3/P4, O1/O2) per qEEG findings. Brainwave Enhancement (528 Hz / Solfeggio Mi) at hypoactive regions. Distinct from anatomical-only laser placement in Profile A/D — this is brainwave-guided.
Appears in: Alexander (14 EEG-site references), Anaya (16), Daryan (6), Abdulrahman (5), Ellis · primarily Profile B and F
Element 3 · Profile B/C/F
Anti-Inflammatory Hybrid Diet
AIP / Paleo / Carnivore / Low-Histamine elimination. More aggressive than standard Paleo (Profile D modal). Multi-week reset structure. Histamine specifically targeted in dopamine-pathway-relevant cases. Strict 14-day Carnivore-Paleo reset in severe Profile F.
Appears in: Daryan, Anaya, Ted, Michael L (heaviest), Lachie · primarily Profile B, F
Element 4 · Profile F
Multi-Phase Structure (Reset → Detox → ESM)
Phase 1: dietary reset (14 days). Phase 2: detox & immune support (sauna + cryotherapy + rebounder + mycotoxin remediation). Phase 3: NeuroSolution ESM Homecare (the standard protocol). Sequencing matters — early-stage neurological work doesn't take in inflamed/dysregulated state.
Appears in: Michael L (full 3-phase), Rhett (implied), Lachie (partial) · Profile F-specific
Element 5 · Profile F
Detox Stack: Sauna + Cryotherapy + Rebounder
Sauna 5×/week. Cryotherapy specifically right-side (contralateral activation for left frontal disinhibition typical of PANS). Rebounder for lymphatic drainage. First sauna routines in pediatric protocols appear here.
Appears in: Ted, Michael L · Profile F primary, Profile B emerging
Element 6 · Profile F
PEMF + Fisher Wallace Cranial E-Stim
PEMF (NeoRhythm) headband for sleep / theta states. Fisher Wallace cranial electrostimulation for OCD/anxiety reduction. Different device class than standard NeuroSage laser+SaeboStim+Rezzimax stack. Suggests Profile F kit needs different device complement.
Appears in: Lachie · also Arsalan adult ABI variant (out of cohort)
Element 7 · Profile E
Separate Milestones Doc
Goals/milestones tracked in a separate parent-facing document, distinct from the prescription. Likely because ABI progress is measured against pre-injury baseline (regaining function) rather than developmental norms (gaining function). Suggests Concierge call needs a "set milestones" deliverable for Profile E that other profiles don't require.
Appears in: Hana, Lily · Profile E-specific
Element 8 · Profile F
Rapid Regulation / Flare Protocol
Explicit "Rapid Regulation Protocol for Stimming & Outbursts" section appended to base prescription. Tells the family what to do when a flare hits — not a separate prescription, but an emergency module of the same one. Distinct from the standard daily protocol.
Appears in: Ellis · Profile F-specific (likely should appear in all F cases)
Why these matter for V1: Each Signature Element should be a tagged, reusable protocol module in the GHL Custom Fields / V2 Hub prescription engine. When concierge classifies a case as Profile B, the engine pulls the standard Profile B template plus relevant Signature Elements (qEEG-driven targeting, anti-inflammatory hybrid diet, possibly vibration-speech pairing). The elements are how the framework stays modular without exploding into hundreds of profile sub-types.

What we learn from the pairs

  • Profile E has the longest time horizons — Briar's 20-month pair and Dana's 8-month pair suggest ABI rehab arcs are measured in years, not weeks. Continuing Care for Profile E probably looks different than for other profiles.
  • Updates preserve ~85% of original prescription — Aisha's 86% line overlap suggests the framework is roughly: keep the daily protocol stable, add or swap specific elements (laser placement, supplements, frequency setting). The protocol is more like a living document than a fresh prescription.
  • Updates often add new technology — Aisha (gamma IR), Ted (sauna), Lachie (basal ganglia laser). Continuing Care may be how new tech gets introduced into existing protocols rather than via initial prescription.
  • Intensity can de-escalate — Rafeeah HIGH → MID. Continuing Care may modally feature intensity reduction, not just protocol modification.
  • Profile F updates sharpen targeting — Lachie update is more specific than initial. PANS evolution may be characterized by tightening from broad anti-inflammatory work to precise D2/basal ganglia targeting as flares resolve.
Out of scope for V1. The 12-week intensive prescription is what V1 ships. Continuing Care subscription and protocol-evolution logic is V2 territory. These signals are noted now so they don't get lost when Dr. Kyle's review happens — they belong in the same conversation as the v0.3 framework.

Capture observable signals

Concierge asks the parent (or watches a video intake):

  • Did your child develop typically and then regress, or have these challenges been present from very early on? (regressive vs. early-onset → routes toward C or F)
  • If regression — was it gradual (weeks/months) or sudden (days)? (sudden → Profile F PANS, gradual → Profile C)
  • Was there an infection, illness, or trigger event before the onset/regression? (yes → Profile F)
  • Is there a known structural insult — birth trauma, NICU, head injury, hypoxic event? (yes → Profile E)
  • Does your child have GI symptoms, food sensitivities, mold exposure history, or known inflammatory conditions? (yes → routes toward B, C, or F)
  • How does your child's posture and movement look? Are they hypotonic, asymmetric, often crawling or walking unusually? (yes → routes toward A or B)
  • What was the most recent ATEC or screening tool score? (severity proxy)
  • How much time per day can your family realistically commit to a home protocol? (intensity capacity)
2

Provisional profile classification

  • Sudden onset + infection trigger + tics/OCD/regression → Profile F (immediate MD-led intake — flare protocol may be needed before standard intake)
  • Known structural insult + focal deficits → Profile E (qEEG strongly recommended at intake; milestones doc deliverable required)
  • Reflex signs + low biomed → Profile A (most common after Profile D)
  • Reflex signs + high biomed + qEEG-able → Profile B (flag for MD-led intake)
  • Gradual regressive history + mold/environmental drivers + few reflex signs → Profile C
  • Mild presentation, verbal, few reflex signs, low biomed → Profile D (modal)
  • Ambiguous → default Profile D, MID intensity, escalate at baseline if Dr. Kyle reclassifies
3

Provisional intensity band

  • Profile A or B → start MID, plan to escalate to HIGH at week 2-3 if family executing well
  • Profile C → start MID; biomedical pathway may run independently in the Continuing Care subscription
  • Profile D → start MID, may reduce to LIGHT if family bandwidth dictates
  • Profile E → start MID — empirically all 4 cohort cases run MID; HIGH possible if region rebuild requires
  • Profile F → severity-driven: severe (multi-phase reset) HIGH, moderate MID, DBX-tier with flare module LIGHT
  • Family-stated bandwidth low → start LIGHT regardless of profile, with explicit understanding the case may need to escalate later
4

Pipeline assignment in GHL

Capture provisional classification in GHL custom fields:

  • profile_provisional = A / B / C / D / E / F
  • intensity_provisional = LIGHT / MID / HIGH
  • laterality_provisional = RB-weak / LB-weak / mixed (default RB-weak unless contraindicated; Profile F often LB-frontal-weak — flag separately)
  • md_review_required = true for Profile B, Profile E, Profile F, ambiguous, or family-flagged complex cases
  • signature_elements = list of applicable Signature Element modules (vibration-speech pairing, qEEG-driven targeting, anti-inflammatory hybrid, multi-phase structure, detox stack, PEMF/Fisher-Wallace, milestones doc, flare protocol)
5

Dr. Kyle confirms or revises at baseline

The provisional classification is the starting hypothesis. Dr. Kyle's first session — using the Brain Exam Checklist (intake instrument) — confirms profile, refines intensity, and locks in BRB laterality based on actual exam. Final classification populates the profile_final, intensity_initial, and laterality_final custom fields and unlocks the Active Treatment pipeline. The Brainstem Checklist (longitudinal instrument) is set up for parent/clinician tracking across the 12-week intensive.

§9 · Operations

Edge cases — the MD-led intake queue

Roughly 20-25% of inbound families won't fit cleanly into the six profiles or will require MD-built prescriptions even when the profile is clear. These cases skip standard routing and go directly to MD-led intake.

Triggers for MD-led intake

Profile B (complex multi-pathway). By definition these cases need both pathways considered together. Provisional classification is fine; the prescription itself must be MD-built.
Profile E (Acquired Brain Injury). Regional targeting requires qEEG findings; milestones doc deliverable requires MD authorship. Cannot be templated.
Profile F (PANS/PANDAS) — all cases. Multi-phase structure, biomedical co-management with Dr. Jared, flare protocol, basal ganglia targeting. Coordination with biomedical workup required at intake. Severity assessment determines whether dietary reset Phase 1 is needed before Phase 3 ESM Homecare.
Boundary cases between Profile B and F. Where presentation suggests qEEG-driven Profile B but biomedical/inflammation history suggests F. Daryan is the current example — pending Dr. Kyle disambiguation.
Significant comorbid medical issues. Seizures (though Duru is in Profile A as seizure stabilization variant), diagnosed mitochondrial disorder, genetic syndromes, recent significant medical events.
Severe / Level 3 / non-verbal presentation. The home model's outcomes scale with engagement; severely impaired children require higher clinician touch than the home model can deliver in V1. May be excluded from V1 pilot or directed to Immersive-only with extended timeline.
Adolescent or older patient. NeuroHome's V1 pediatric pilot focuses on ages 2–10. Older children/adolescents have different developmental priorities and family dynamics.
Family-flagged ambiguity. Concierge unable to confidently classify after intake. Better to MD-review than to start with the wrong profile.
International / co-managed cases. Dubai cohort co-managed with Dr. Manteghi (Alexander, Anaya). Different intake length (90 min vs 30) and concierge handoff. Operational pattern, not clinical, but routes through MD queue.

What MD-led intake produces

For these cases, Dr. Kyle (or the MD designee) reviews the case directly during a longer intake session (60–90 min vs. the standard 30-min concierge call). Output: same GHL custom fields populated, but with explicit MD-authored notes on what's atypical and why. These cases also trigger:

  • Heightened concierge monitoring in weeks 1-2
  • Earlier mid-point review (week 4 instead of week 6)
  • Family-facing acknowledgment: "your case is more complex than our standard protocol — Dr. Kyle is personally building your prescription"
  • For Profile F: explicit flare-readiness counseling with the family at intake, plus the Rapid Regulation Protocol module bolted onto the standard prescription from day one
  • For Profile E: milestones-to-achieve document delivered alongside prescription; reassessment cadence aligned with rehab arc rather than 12-week developmental window
