The three-layer architecture

Every patient carries more than just a bucket assignment. The operational signature stacks three frames:

Layer 1 · MasterDevelopmental Pyramid
Layer 2 · ClusterPhenotype Engine (this doc)
Layer 3 · BiologyMechanism Scaffolding

The phenotype engine constrains classification, the developmental pyramid orients intervention sequencing, and the mechanism scaffolding (drawn from Brock 2025 and aligned literature) provides biological story. Foundational, Sensory-Motor, and Birth Trauma sit primarily in the developmental layer. Environmental, Hyperimmune, Acquired Regression, and Severe Dysregulation pull heavily on the mechanism layer.

What this profile is

The Foundational child has mild developmental delays — speech, motor, attention — but presents without the systemic or immune red flags that drive higher-severity buckets. Reflexes are retained or marginally integrated. Sensory processing has soft signs. The gut is generally healthy, sleep is normal, the child engages socially and makes eye contact. This is the modal NeuroHome family by case count, and the bucket that maps most cleanly to standard developmental neuroscience without requiring inflammatory, immune, or environmental scaffolding.

For parents: "Your child is developing along a typical pathway with mild delays in foundational skills. We're not looking at an inflammatory or immune problem. We're rebuilding the neurological foundations underneath the symptoms you're seeing — reflexes, balance, eye-tracking, coordination — so that higher skills can stabilize."

What this profile is

The Sensory-Motor child has dyspraxia, coordination deficits, retained primitive reflexes, and sensory processing issues that read as the dominant clinical signature. Unlike Foundational, the reflex burden is heavier and the coordination signs are more pronounced — toe walking, clumsiness, motion sensitivity, poor handwriting. Unlike Birth Trauma, there's no perinatal or structural etiology. Unlike higher-severity profiles, the systemic and behavioral picture remains stable. This is arguably the most scientifically defensible bucket in the framework — dyspraxia, vestibular dysfunction, cerebellar timing, and sensory integration all have substantial developmental neuroscience evidence behind them.

For parents: "Your child's nervous system has gaps in the foundational sensory-motor wiring — the parts that handle balance, coordination, timing, and body awareness. That's why we see the toe walking, the clumsiness, the difficulty with handwriting and sports. We rebuild that wiring through targeted physical input. It's not a behavioral problem and it's not in their head — it's in their body."

What this profile is

The Birth Trauma / Structural child has identifiable focal neurological deficits matching specific brain regions — different from the diffuse developmental delay of Foundational. The etiology may be perinatal (forceps, vacuum, NICU, hypoxia, cord complication), acquired neurological (head trauma, surgical event), or genetic-structural (PCH, PTCD). The clinical signature is asymmetry, cranial nerve dysfunction, feeding/swallowing history, and motor planning deficits. qEEG localizes underactive regions and drives the prescription.

For parents: "Something happened to your child's nervous system around birth (or in early development) that affected specific brain regions. We see the effects in their movement, their feeding history, their motor planning. Our work targets those regions specifically and rebuilds function against the baseline of where they were before. Progress is measured against pre-injury function, not standard developmental milestones."

What this profile is

Emotional volatility. Severe primitive reflex retention. Involuntary movements. Brainstem-driven presentation. The autonomic nervous system is unstable — poor sweating, dysautonomia signs, weak vagal regulation. GI inflammation. Food sensitivities. Poor detoxification. Chronic immune activation. This is the bucket where everything is hard at once. MD-led intake is mandatory. Routed to clinician-built prescription, not template.
